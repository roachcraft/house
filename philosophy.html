philosophy of a cockroach

leaf 01 / gathered things

i have a habit of bringing things home.

scraps of fabric. discarded words. curious objects. things that caught my attention for reasons i can't always explain.

sometimes i see what something could become. sometimes i have absolutely no idea. sometimes i just think it's neat.

i like looking at familiar things as though i've only just arrived here. before anyone has explained what they're for, what they're worth, or what i'm supposed to think about them.

there's a particular kind of wonder in not knowing any better.

i don't mean that everything is secretly extraordinary. some things really are just garbage. but i like finding out for myself.

once you start questioning what something is for, it gets a shitload harder to see what it can be.

leaf 02 / what gets discarded

humans spend an awful lot of time deciding what's worth keeping.

what's useful. what's beautiful. what's appropriate. what's welcome. what's garbage.

humans make similar decisions about creatures, places, & most confusingly, other humans.

i'm not entirely convinced us humans know what the fuck we're talking about.

i've never been especially comfortable with how easily things are thrown away. particularly facing the inevitable waste my own existence produces in a world already struggling beneath the weight of it.

there's guilt in that. frustration, too, when wanton disregard & explotation is built into the everyday systems we live within.

to be clear: i'm under no illusion that
sewing, painting, or hot-gluing garbage together
will fix any of this. that's not the tea.

there's something worthwhile in interrupting the process; in picking up something already written off & asking what else it might be.

maybe it isn't finished being something yet.

i suppose i've been susceptible to that idea for awhile.

leaf 03 / making do

a cockroach learns pretty early to make do with whatever is available.

sometimes creativity was play. sometimes it was necessity. more often than not, those things were impossible to separate.

i was a curious kid with a lot of ideas & not much to work with. so i improvised. took things apart. made something out of whatever i could find.

there's nothing to be romanticized about experiencing limited access, but i do think it left me with a particular affection for possibility.

i suppose the habit stuck.

somewhere along the way, though, making things became surprisingly difficult.

an idea could be examined, questioned, planned, revised, & reconsidered until there was very little chance of it ever becoming an actual thing.

an impressive amount of work could go into making absolutely nothing.

eventually, through the rip tide, i thought there might be another way to go about it.

leaf 04 / getting out of my own way

not necessarily an evidence-based method. certainly not a more organized one.

just a loophole to begin before i'd managed to talk myself out of beginning.

so i've been trying to let the materials do the talking.

starting before i know exactly what i'm making. letting one piece suggest another. allowing things to become other things without insisting that i understand the outcome first.

sometimes that means following an impulse that doesn't make much sense. sometimes it means giving an idea enough room to be stupid.

mostly it means attempting to get out of the way.

roachcraft is, in part, a little workaround for all of this.

i gave the making a name. a verb. a place to live. a little distance from the spicy part of my brain that wants to hold a committee meeting over every impulse.

now there's somewhere for things to happen before they're entirely understood.

it's not a perfect system. but more things do seem to be happening.

& i suppose it's no accident that the whole thing was born from a cockroach.

leaf 05 / the cockroach

consider the cockroach.

an extraordinarily successful little creature with a remarkably poor reputation.

not particularly sparkly. rarely invited anywhere. frequently misunderstood. exceptionally difficult to get rid of.

there's something deeply appealing about that.

cockroaches persist in circumstances that would be the end of many other creatures. not necessarily through anything spectacular, but through adaptability & a rather inconvenient refusal to stop existing.

there's something familiar in being misunderstood, underestimated, or made to feel unwelcome.

also something worth remembering about the difference between being undesirable & being undeserving.

of course, surviving difficult circumstances doesn't make those circumstances good. sometimes survival is simply what happens because disappearing wasn't an acceptable option.

still here. still poking around. still getting into things.

cockroaches are resistance & resiliance. they're punk as hell.

but survival isn't the only reason i like being a cockroach.

leaf 06 / what freedom!

there's also the rather wonderful matter of expectations.

when someone hears "cockroach", those expectations are generally already on the floor.

what freedom!

there's no particular pressure to be impressive, shiny, respectable, or even especially good at anything.

a cockroach is free to be strange, ridiculous,
to crawl around on the concrete & behind the furniture.

you can try something without first establishing whether you're any good at it.

and if the whole thing turns out spectacularly stupid, well.

that's literally perfect.

i think some of that freedom has found its way into the making (the roachcrafting, if you will).

when things don't have to become what they're supposed to become, there's a lot more room for them to become something interesting.

& once you allow for that kind of nonsense, the line between silly and serious starts getting wonderfully blurry.

leaf 07 / further considerations

i've not found that line especially useful anyway.

some things are jokes. some things are deeply personal. often they're both.

sometimes an idea i have is so profoundly stupid that it really must come to be.

sometimes things mean absolutely nothing.

that's allowed, too.

i'm drawn to curiosity, resourcefulness, transformation, & the peculiar things that manage to persist despite everything.

i'm interested in questioning the limitations we've been taught to accept, including the ones we place on ourselves and each other.

i care about what we throw away, what we overlook, and who gets dismissed.

i also think there's enormous value in exercising our free will to be abnormal.

these things don't feel contradictory to me. taking something seriously doesn't mean we have to stop playing with it.

sometimes play is precisely what allows us to see something differently in the first place.

which leaves me with a few fairly unhelpful conclusions.

leaf 08 / a work in progress

not everything needs to be useful.
not everything needs to be beautiful.
not everything needs to make sense.

& things rarely turn out how they're supposed to.

this isn't necessarily a problem.

i try to remember that
& since i need to practice,
i built somewhere to do it.

that's what roachcraft is, really.

part collection, part experiment, part evidence that i started something before i could talk myself out of it.

there are finished things here. unfinished things. things that were meant to be something else. probably things that shouldn't be here at all.

i'm not entirely sure what this place is becoming, but it will inevitably grow a life of its own.

you're welcome to wander, poke around, turn things over, & get lost a little.

i'll be doing much the same.

it's a work in progress.
so am i.
