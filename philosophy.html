philosophy of a cockroach

leaf 01 / gathered things

i have a habit of bringing things home.

scraps of fabric. discarded words. curious objects. things that caught my attention for reasons i can't always explain.

sometimes i see what something could become. sometimes i have absolutely no idea. sometimes i just think it's neat.

i like looking at familiar things as though i've only just arrived here. before anyone has explained what they're for, what they're worth, or what i'm supposed to think about them.

there's a particular kind of wonder in not knowing any better.

we spend an awful lot of time deciding what's worth keeping.

what's useful. what's beautiful. what's appropriate. what's welcome. what's garbage.

we make similar decisions about creatures, places, and people.

i'm not entirely convinced any of us know what the fuck we're talking about.

i've never been especially comfortable with how easily things are thrown away. especially knowing how much waste my own existence produces in a world already struggling beneath the weight of it.

there's guilt in that. and frustration, too, when so much waste is built into the systems we live within.

i'm under no illusion that making things out of garbage will fix any of this.

but i find something worthwhile in interrupting the process. in picking up something already written off and asking what else it might be.

perhaps it isn't finished being something yet.

leaf 02 / making do

i learned pretty early to make do with whatever was available.

sometimes creativity was play. sometimes it was necessity. more often than not, those things were impossible to separate.

i learned to improvise. to take things apart. to find possibilities in circumstances that didn't appear to offer many.

i suppose the habit stuck.

but somewhere along the way, making things also became surprisingly difficult.

an idea could be examined, questioned, planned, revised, and reconsidered until there was very little chance of it ever becoming an actual thing.

an impressive amount of work could go into making absolutely nothing.

so i've been trying something else.

letting the materials have a say.

starting before i know exactly what i'm making. allowing things to become other things without insisting that i understand the outcome first.

sometimes that means giving an idea enough room to be stupid. sometimes it means getting out of my own way.

roachcraft is, in part, an attempt to make that possible.

i gave the making a name. a place to live. a little distance from the part of my brain that wants to hold a committee meeting over every impulse.

now there's somewhere for things to happen before they're entirely understood.

it's not a perfect system.

but more things seem to be happening.

leaf 03 / the cockroach

consider the cockroach.

an extraordinarily successful little creature with a remarkably poor reputation.

not particularly sparkly. rarely invited anywhere. frequently misunderstood. exceptionally difficult to get rid of.

there's something deeply appealing about that.

cockroaches persist in circumstances that would be the end of many other creatures. not necessarily through anything spectacular, but through adaptability and a rather inconvenient refusal to stop existing.

there's something familiar in being misunderstood, underestimated, or made to feel unwelcome.

and something freeing about becoming a creature for whom expectations are already on the floor.

what freedom!

you can be strange. you can be ridiculous. you can crawl around on the floor making faces.

you can try things without first establishing whether you're any good at them.

you can simply be a cockroach.

still here. still poking around. still getting into things.

i find these admirable pursuits.

leaf 04 / further considerations

sometimes things get taken apart and made into other things. sometimes they're perfectly fine as they are. sometimes an idea is so profoundly stupid that it really must come to be.

some things are jokes. some things are deeply personal. often they're the same thing.

sometimes things mean absolutely nothing.

that's allowed, too.

i'm interested in curiosity, resourcefulness, transformation, and the peculiar things that manage to persist despite everything.

i'm interested in questioning the limitations we've been taught to accept, including the ones we place on ourselves.

and i believe there's enormous value in exercising our free will to be ridiculous.

not everything needs to be useful.
not everything needs to be beautiful.
not everything needs to make sense.

& things rarely turn out how they're supposed to.

this isn't necessarily a problem.

roachcraft is my living corner of the internet.

part collection, part experiment, part evidence that something has been going on.

a place to make things, collect things, try things, change my mind, and document whatever comes of it.

not everything here is finished. not everything here is important. some things probably shouldn't be here at all.

you're welcome to wander, poke around, and draw your own conclusions.

i'll be doing much the same.

it's a work in progress.
so am i.
